Part 1

Two Kinds of Time Words

Some time words already know exactly when they mean. Other time words come back again and again — every year, every week, every day — so on their own, we don't know which one you're talking about. That second kind needs a little help. That help is 的时候.

Type A · Only happens once

定点词

These words already point to one exact moment. Nothing else to add — just say it.

昨天 yesterday 今天 today 明天 tomorrow 去年 last year 今年 this year 现在 now 刚才 just now
昨天我去了北京。
Yesterday I went to Beijing. — no marker needed.
Type B · Happens every year / week / day

循环词

These words repeat. Said alone, we don't know which one you mean — so we usually add 的时候 to point at "this one time."

秋天 autumn 圣诞节 Christmas 8点 8 o'clock 周二 Tuesday 2020年 the year 2020
秋天的时候，我去了北京。
One specific autumn — I went to Beijing then.
Part 2

Ask Yourself One Question

"If I say this word all by itself, does everyone instantly know which exact moment I mean?"
YES → say it plain

昨天 · 今天 · 明天 · 去年 · 今年 · 现在
No 的时候 needed.

NO, it happens again and again → add 的时候

秋天 · 圣诞节 · 8点 · 周二
的时候 anchors it to "this one time."

Small note: 的时候 is a bit like the English words "in / at / on" — but it's not exactly the same job. In Chinese, time words can normally stand alone right before the verb, with nothing added at all (that's just how Chinese sentences work). 的时候 is an extra word we add on top, to say "this was that one specific moment" — more like storytelling flavor than a required grammar piece.
Part 3 · Special Case

昨天下午 — Can Go Either Way

What happens when you mix a Type A word with a Type B word? Like 昨天 (yesterday) + 下午 (afternoon)? The Type A part already locks down the exact day — so the whole phrase is safe. You can leave 的时候 out, or add it for a "telling a story" feeling.

昨天下午我去了北京。 — plain, states a fact
昨天下午的时候，我去了北京。 — storytelling tone
